% ===================== 1. ABSTRACT / MOTIVATION =====================
\section{Motivation and thesis}

\subsection{The one-paragraph version}

Build a representation of galaxy images \textbf{unsupervised} with a JEPA, then use the Galaxy
Zoo labels \textbf{only as a read-out key} --- to name and test directions the representation
already learned, never to train it. The labels cannot reshape the encoder's geometry; they can
only \emph{misname or blur a read-out direction}. The experiment thus \textbf{moves label noise
out of representation-learning and into a measurement stage} where it is inspectable and
controllable. The scientific question, per feature: is the human morphological concept a
\emph{linearly nameable direction}, an \emph{entangled} or \emph{nonlinear} one, or
\emph{absent} from the image information at this resolution?

\subsection{Why this, why now --- the v1\,$\rightarrow$\,v2 arc}

\textbf{v1's finding, restated.} Galaxy-Zoo-Classifier (the undergraduate dissertation) trained
deep CNNs and Vision Transformers on Galaxy Zoo 2 crowdsourced labels, treating the volunteer
votes as ground truth. Its central, slightly uncomfortable result: the models performed
\emph{to the same ability as the volunteers}, and --- crucially --- \emph{replicated the
volunteers' confusion}. On the hard questions (bulge shape, winding, arm count) the model
confusion matrices mirrored the human ones. The diagnosis: the \textbf{labels} are the
bottleneck. A supervised model trained to reproduce noisy votes can do no better than the votes
themselves; it inherits not just the signal but the noise, including the systematic confusions.
Two ViT failure modes made this stark (diss $\S$8): on the confused questions the ViT learned
to \emph{always predict zero} (underfitting --- it could not extract a feature from the noisy
labels), while ResNet50 learned to predict the \emph{average optimal answer} (hedging against
confusion). Both are symptoms of the same disease: the labels did not contain clean enough
signal to learn from.

\textbf{The structural problem v2 fixes.} Supervised training couples two things that ought to
be separate: \emph{learning what a galaxy looks like} and \emph{fitting the (noisy) votes}.
Because the loss is computed against the votes, vote-noise flows back through the gradients and
\emph{bends the encoder's weights} --- the confusion is baked into the representation. JEPA
breaks the coupling: the encoder is trained only on images (predicting masked-region embeddings
from context) and never sees a label. Labels enter only afterwards, through a probe that cannot
touch the frozen encoder. The noise is thus \emph{relocated} (\S\ref{sec:method}), not removed.

\textbf{v1's own reconnaissance feeds v2.} The dissertation did not merely note the confusion
--- it \emph{characterised} it. The $\S$5.2.1 correlation analysis (Figs 18--19) documented,
per question, which distinctions volunteers agreed on (edge-on, bar, spiral, odd --- strong
negative correlations between mutually-exclusive answers) and which they did not (bulge shape,
winding, arm count --- near-zero off-diagonal structure, i.e.\ ``great confusion''). It even
flagged specific cross-feature confusions with physical character: ``Disk Edge On'' correlating
with ``Cigar'' (Fig 17b), suggesting volunteers could not tell an edge-on disk from a
cigar-shaped elliptical. That observation is the seed of this design's confound taxonomy
(\S\ref{sec:confound}). v1 saw \emph{that} the features were confused and began to see
\emph{why}; v2 has the instrument to measure the why directly.

\textbf{The arc, in one line.} v1 asked \emph{``can we match the volunteers?''}; v2 asks
\emph{``can we stop needing them --- and use them only to read off what the images already
taught the model?''} The motivating pressure is the same one v1 opened with: forthcoming surveys
(LSST/Rubin: ${\sim}150$\,PB, ${\sim}10^{10}$ galaxies) will produce far more imaging than
crowdsourcing can ever label. Self-supervised learning on the unlabelled deluge is the field's
answer --- and this project asks what such a representation actually \emph{knows} about human
morphological concepts, before any label is applied.

% ===================== 2. THE FRAMING DECISION =====================
\section{The framing decision: A-spine, B-payoff}\label{sec:framing}


This is the single most important architectural decision about the paper, and it governs how
every deferred call below is held.

\subsection{The two framings}

\textbf{Framing A --- the method.} \emph{``Can a label-free representation recover human
morphological concepts?''} Contribution is methodological: build a JEPA, freeze it, probe it,
produce a per-feature nameability ladder. Subject = the representation. Galaxy Zoo is the
\emph{yardstick}. Safe, always-true, promiseable in the abstract regardless of results.

\textbf{Framing B --- the taxonomy audit.} \emph{``Which human morphological concepts are real,
separable structure in galaxy images --- using a label-free representation as the
instrument?''} Subject = the concepts themselves. Galaxy Zoo's decision tree is the \emph{object
under audit}. Bigger, more original --- but a much higher defensibility bar, load-bearing on
results not yet in hand.

\begin{decidedbox}
\textbf{Don't pick --- A is the spine, B is the earned payoff.} The paper's \emph{stated
contribution and spine} is Framing~A (method + nameability ladder + controls): unimpeachable,
true whatever the results. Framing~B is the \emph{interpretive arc} --- set up early, paid off
in the discussion \emph{if and only if the results earn it}. Same discipline as the
uncertainty-geometry hedge: backbone on what's always true; let the bold claims be earned
headlines, never load-bearing premises.
\end{decidedbox}

\subsection{The decision rule (pre-registrable)}

\begin{deferredbox}
\textbf{B is earned iff} the entanglement on the headline confused features is consistent
\emph{across objectives} (JEPA \emph{and} MAE \emph{and} MoCo) \emph{and across resolutions}
(16$\times$16 \emph{and} 8$\times$8) --- and, added by this design, \emph{across the
confound-fingerprint axes} (\S\ref{sec:confound}). Consistent $\Rightarrow$ likely the
data/taxonomy (B). Inconsistent across objectives $\Rightarrow$ A-only, and the inconsistency
is itself a finding (objective-specific entanglement). \textbf{Committed at analysis time, not
now.}
\end{deferredbox}

\subsection{Three concrete design consequences}


\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{MAE/MoCo baselines: load-bearing for the payoff.} Promoted from
``robustness nice-to-have'' to ``the test that decides whether the paper makes the strong or
weak entanglement claim.'' They move up the priority order --- wanted on the headline confused
features.
\item \textbf{Entanglement figure (Fig.~3) becomes comparative.} Not ``here's the
entanglement'' but ``here's whether it is consistent across objectives and resolutions,''
because that consistency adjudicates A-vs-B.
\item \textbf{Discussion written in two branches, committed at analysis time.} Consistency
holds $\rightarrow$ the B payoff; doesn't $\rightarrow$ honest A-only. Either way the paper is
complete and honest.
\end{enumerate}

% ===================== 3. CORE METHOD =====================
\section{Core method: labels as a read-out key}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The methodological heart}


The encoder is built unsupervised and \textbf{frozen}. It carves the latent space along
whatever axes of variation actually exist in galaxy images. \textbf{The labels never bend those
axes} --- they are consulted only afterwards, to ask \emph{``which pre-existing direction lines
up with what humans called a bar / bulge / edge-on disk?''} A mislabelled galaxy can
\emph{misname or blur a read-out direction} (a local, inspectable measurement error); it
\emph{cannot reshape the encoder's geometry} the way it deformed v1's weights. Noise is not
eliminated --- it is \textbf{relocated} from representation-learning to measurement, where it
is bounded with controls.

\begin{openbox}
``Feature = direction'' is a \textbf{working hypothesis}, not an assumption. The science is
\emph{which} features are nameable directions and which are not (the ladder). Held honest by
the controls (\S\ref{sec:controls}).
\end{openbox}

\subsection{Stage 1 --- representation (label-free)}

\dec{} \textbf{I-JEPA} (Assran et al.\ 2023): a context-encoder ViT, an EMA target encoder
(the anti-collapse mechanism --- the target weights are an exponential moving average of the
context weights, so they cannot be trivially matched), and a predictor. The model predicts the
\emph{embeddings} of masked image blocks from the embeddings of a context block, with the loss
an MSE in \emph{latent} space. \textbf{There is no pixel reconstruction} --- this is the axis
that distinguishes I-JEPA from a masked autoencoder (MAE), and it matters for the science: a
latent-prediction objective need not preserve pixel-level detail the way a reconstruction
objective must, so the two objectives may organise the manifold differently. That difference is
exactly what the cross-objective control exploits (\S\ref{sec:ladder}). Reuse v1's
$256{\times}256 \rightarrow 256\times(16{\times}16)$ patchification as the tokeniser.

Galaxy-specific design (\dec{} for the minimal run; later ablations noted):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Masking must target the galaxy.} An SDSS cutout is mostly black sky; block-masking
it uniformly wastes prediction budget on empty regions. Bias mask sampling toward the galaxy,
reusing v1's average-image bounding box.
\item \textbf{Rotational symmetry is a free inductive bias.} Galaxies have no canonical
orientation. Start with rotation/reflection \emph{augmentation} (simplest, de-risks the minimal
run). The principled extension --- an \textbf{E(2)-equivariant ViT} that bakes in continuous
rotation/reflection --- reshapes the encoder geometry, so establish the vanilla-ViT ladder
first, then ablate. (Precedent: Lens-JEPA, physics-informed, beats supervised + vanilla I-JEPA.)
\item \textbf{Resolution floor.} The $16{\times}16$ token grid ($256 \div 16$) sets the
spatial-resolution floor: the model cannot attend below a patch, and the patch-embed linear map
compresses sub-patch structure. Thin arms / tight winding may live below that floor --- so an
\textbf{8$\times$8-patch ablation} is the R4 control (\S\ref{sec:ladder}), distinguishing
\emph{absent from the pixels} from \emph{under-resolved by the tokeniser}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stage 2 --- readout = measurement (the heart)}

The encoder is \textbf{frozen throughout}. Stage 2 is a \emph{measurement protocol}, not a
training stage; labels never reach the encoder.

\begin{decidedbox}
\textbf{Concept direction --- canonically an L2-regularised logistic probe.} A feature is
linearly nameable if an L2-regularised logistic probe on frozen embeddings reaches
statistically significant held-out AUC \emph{and} selectivity over control labels; the
unit-normalised probe weight vector is the concept direction. Logistic over mean-difference
because it gives a proper decision axis, AUC, regularisation, calibration, and class-imbalance
handling. \textbf{Keep the mean-difference (CAV) vector as a robustness check} --- and note they
answer slightly different questions: logistic gives the \emph{discriminative} direction (best
separating axis given everything else), mean-difference the \emph{marginal} one. Their
\emph{disagreement} is itself an entanglement signal.
\end{decidedbox}

\textbf{Walk the ladder} (\S\ref{sec:ladder}): the linear probe first (defines ``clean
direction''); then MLP/$k$-NN \emph{only} to distinguish entangled-but-present (R3) from absent
(R4), and \emph{only} with the controls (\S\ref{sec:controls}).

\textbf{Confidence does double duty} --- informing the probe and the evaluation, never the
encoder --- but the two uses must stay \emph{separate axes} to avoid circularity:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{As a probe target (one axis):} regress the vote fraction, or weight the probe loss
by confidence. Useful --- but this axis \textbf{cannot} be used to test uncertainty geometry
(recovering the vote fraction along an axis you trained on it is a tautology).
\item \emph{As uncertainty geometry --- the headline (a different axis):} train the concept axis
on high-consensus extremes only, then project the held-out ambiguous middle and test whether
margin distance ranks vote fractions. The axis never saw the graded values it is asked to
reproduce (\S\ref{sec:uncertainty}).
\end{itemize}

\opn{} \textbf{Fine-tuning} is a subordinate comparison only --- unfreezing buys a few points
but lets vote-noise bend the encoder again, re-introducing v1's coupling. A contrast with eyes
open, never the main line.

% ===================== 4. THE NAMEABILITY LADDER =====================
\section{The nameability ladder}\label{sec:ladder}


For any feature, ``can the labels name it?'' has four answers, each a \emph{different result}:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt]
\item[\textbf{R1}] \textbf{Clean linear direction.} A single vector whose projection tracks the
feature. \emph{(Bet: smooth/featured, edge-on.)}
\item[\textbf{R2}] \textbf{Entangled linear direction.} The axis exists but isn't orthogonal ---
moving along ``bar'' drags bulge-size or inclination. Nameable, not independent.
\item[\textbf{R3}] \textbf{Nonlinear manifold.} No single vector; a small MLP/$k$-NN decodes it.
\emph{Candidate} for ``present but tangled'' --- but see the guardrail.
\item[\textbf{R4}] \textbf{Not recoverable.} No probe finds it.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{openbox}
\textbf{Guardrail on R3.} A nonlinear probe succeeding does \emph{not} by itself mean ``present
but tangled'' --- it may have found a \emph{spurious correlate} (survey, redshift, size,
brightness, SNR, label prior). R3 is only credible with the controls battery
(\S\ref{sec:controls}).
\end{openbox}

\subsection{The diagnosis v1 couldn't give}

Mapping the confused features onto the ladder is the paper's core result:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item Come back as \textbf{clean directions (R1)} $\rightarrow$ supports the strongest version of
the v1 thesis: the information was present in the pixels, but the \emph{supervised objective and
noisy vote targets} failed to extract it cleanly. (Note: \emph{failed to extract cleanly} ---
not ``purely the labels''; architecture, splits, imbalance, thresholding, augmentation and
resolution could also contribute.)
\item Need a \textbf{(controlled) nonlinear probe (R3)} $\rightarrow$ present but tangled;
supervised training never had a chance through noisy labels.
\item \textbf{Nothing decodes them (R4)} $\rightarrow$ genuinely not in the pixels at this
resolution; never a labels problem.
\end{itemize}
Which rung each confused feature lands on \textbf{is the paper}.

\subsection{What sets the rung (besides the feature)}

The rung is \textbf{not intrinsic to the feature} --- it is a joint property of
\emph{(feature $\times$ SSL objective $\times$ architecture/resolution $\times$ probe)}. Four
confounds, each with its control:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Probe capacity / spurious correlates} $\rightarrow$ the controls battery
(\S\ref{sec:controls}). A nonlinear probe can win on size / brightness / survey, not morphology.
\item \textbf{Probe-target circularity} $\rightarrow$ train the axis on high-consensus
\emph{extremes} only, test on the held-out ambiguous middle (\S\ref{sec:uncertainty}). Regressing
the vote fraction then ``recovering'' it is a tautology.
\item \textbf{SSL-objective geometry} $\rightarrow$ run the \emph{same ladder} on JEPA vs
contrastive vs MAE. JEPA's latent-MSE objective may not flatten semantic manifolds the way
contrastive's push-pull does, so an R3 result in JEPA could be R1-in-contrastive. Cross-objective
comparison separates \emph{intrinsic to the images} from \emph{artefact of this objective}. (This
is the same machinery that adjudicates Framing~B, \S\ref{sec:framing}.)
\item \textbf{Patch size / resolution} $\rightarrow$ the 8$\times$8 ablation. Thin arms / tight
winding living below the token grid can read as R4 (absent) when they are merely under-resolved.
This is the R4 control, as the baselines are the R3 control.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Attribution is the whole game}: a rung means something about \emph{the images} only once
these are held down.

% ===================== 5. UNCERTAINTY GEOMETRY =====================
\section{Uncertainty geometry --- the high-beta headline}\label{sec:uncertainty}


Not ``does the axis classify bars?'' but ``does \emph{distance} along the unsupervised axis
reproduce the volunteer \emph{vote fraction}?'' --- a stronger diagnostic than AUC (which can be
high on a brittle boundary).

\begin{decidedbox}
\textbf{The non-circular protocol.} Fit the axis on \textbf{high-consensus extremes only} (a
binary logistic probe on $v>0.8$ vs $v<0.2$). Hold the ambiguous middle ($0.2<v<0.8$) out of
estimation entirely. Then project those unseen middle galaxies and test (Spearman) whether
their margin distance ranks their human vote fraction. An axis trained only on confident
examples ordering the ambiguous ones by a human uncertainty it never saw $\Rightarrow$ the
geometry genuinely aligns with the human ambiguity gradient, not by construction.
\end{decidedbox}

The meaning-flip this enables: v1's confusion matrices mirrored the volunteers' \emph{because
trained on the votes}; if the latent geometry reproduces that uncertainty \emph{without ever
being trained on votes}, the ambiguity is a \emph{real property of the images}, not a labelling
artefact. Same observation, opposite conclusion.

\begin{deferredbox}
Uncertainty geometry is the highest-upside result \emph{and} the most likely to come out null.
The paper is backboned on the ladder + controls + v1 comparison; this is the high-beta
headline, not a load-bearing premise.
\end{deferredbox}

% ===================== 6. THE CONFOUND TAXONOMY (Framing B mechanism) =====================
\section{The confound taxonomy --- Framing B's mechanism}\label{sec:confound}

\begin{deferredbox}
\textbf{Status of this entire section.} This specifies the \emph{mechanism} for Framing~B's
earned payoff. It is held as an \emph{interpretive lens pending results}, \textbf{not the
paper's spine} (which remains Framing~A, \S\ref{sec:framing}). The three-confound taxonomy is
what B \emph{might deliver if the results earn it} --- the same A-spine/B-payoff discipline,
one level deeper. It adds a measurement capability (inclination conditioning) but does not
revise the locked experimental design (\S\ref{sec:harness}). Leading with this story must not
read as committing to it.
\end{deferredbox}


\subsection{The organising idea}

A label-free encoder never sees the votes during training, so it is not constrained to
reproduce human confusion. This lets us ask, per confused feature: is the confusion in the
\textbf{data} (the encoder is also confused $\rightarrow$ a genuine information limit) or in the
\textbf{humans} (the info is in the pixels; the encoder separates what people cannot)? Grounded
in v1's own correlation analysis, human confusion splits into (at least) three physical causes,
each with a distinct fingerprint across the \textbf{(inclination $\times$ imaging-depth)} plane.

\subsection{Confound 1 --- projection (viewing-angle information loss)}

\textbf{Case:} edge-on disk $\leftrightarrow$ cigar elliptical. v1 data: Edge-on-Disk
$\times$ Cigar $= +0.83$ (strong positive correlation between features on \emph{different} tree
branches --- the edge-on 2D image cannot disambiguate them). \textbf{Fingerprint:}
angle-dependent, imaging-depth-\emph{invariant} (no resolution recovers 3D lost to projection).
\textbf{Test:} condition on inclination.

\subsection{Confound 2 --- resolution \emph{or} semantic ambiguity}

\textbf{Cases:} arm-count, winding, bulge-shape (near-zero off-diagonal structure in v1's
per-question matrices). Two sub-causes the imaging-depth axis \emph{distinguishes}:
\emph{resolution} (info in the sky, SDSS can't resolve it $\rightarrow$ \emph{improves} with
deeper imaging) vs \emph{semantic} (the human concept has no sharp boundary $\rightarrow$
imaging depth does nothing). \opn{} Likely per-feature split: arm-count partly semantic
(the only non-zero off-diagonal is 3$\leftrightarrow$4 arms $= 0.16$); winding more purely
resolution; bulge-shape resolution-or-subtle. \textbf{Test:} recoverability vs imaging depth,
per feature.

\subsection{Confound 3 --- genuine co-occurrence vs artefactual correlation}

\begin{deferredbox}
\textbf{Hypothesis --- unconfirmed.} \textbf{Case:} bar $+$ spiral structure (Bar $\times$
2-arms $= +0.56$). Consistent with real astrophysical co-occurrence \emph{or} with artefact
(clearly-featured galaxies get confident yes-votes downstream). This is the method's
\textbf{hard case}, not a settled control: if the encoder shows bar+arms entangled, the
entanglement might be \emph{correct} (physics), not a representation limit. Adjudicated by
invariance to angle \emph{and} depth plus the astrophysical literature. Cannot be decided until
the measurement exists.
\end{deferredbox}

\subsection{Why the confused questions are kept, not reduced away}

v1's reduction dropped the confused questions out of frustration --- but v1 was a
\emph{supervised classifier trained on the confused labels}, so it inherited the confusion by
construction. v2's label-free encoder has no such constraint, so the confused questions are
\textbf{precisely where v2 can beat v1}. Keep the full tree.

% ===================== 7. THE FEATURE-SCHEME EXPERIMENT =====================
\section{The feature-scheme experiment}\label{sec:scheme}


\subsection{Conditional-population probing --- as a comparison, not a fixed mask}

\dec{} Each feature is only well-defined within the population that reaches its question
(boxy-bulge is meaningless for a no-bulge galaxy --- v1's insight: ``Q4 cannot be yes and Q7
cannot be no for the same galaxy''). \textbf{But do not hard-mask the ``incoherent'' galaxies
away} --- a no-bulge galaxy carrying boxy-bulge votes is a \emph{measurement of human
disagreement}, and masking it pre-imposes the tree's logic before testing whether it holds
(circular). Instead, probe each feature across \emph{different} population definitions (full vs
consensus-conditional) and \textbf{compare}; study the off-population galaxies as their own
object (concentrated $=$ systematic confusion $=$ finding; scattered $=$ noise). Reuse the
\texttt{data/splits.py} firewall machinery.

\subsection{The two schemes}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Scheme 1 --- full tree (37 answers, per-bucket)}, each in its conditional
population. Honest baseline; expected weak on the confused features (echoes v1 $=$ a finding).
BY family $\approx 37$. \textbf{Power confound:} per-bucket deep-feature weakness is confounded
between genuine-absence and split-sample ($\sim$9{,}870 spirals $\div$ 6 arm-buckets
$\approx$ 1{,}600 each) --- Scheme~1 alone cannot distinguish; do not read per-bucket weakness
as ``absent.''
\item \textbf{Scheme 2 --- reduced/smart (diss-style)}: graded questions $\rightarrow$ one
graded axis each; binary well-posed $\rightarrow$ one binary feature; odd-subtypes exploratory.
BY family $\approx 10$--$13$. \textbf{Also a power diagnostic:} arm-count-as-axis on the full
$\sim$9{,}870 recoverable but per-bucket isn't $\Rightarrow$ the per-bucket failure was power.
\end{itemize}

\dec{} \textbf{The comparison is a result.} Same ladder both ways $\Rightarrow$ reduction
cosmetic; differ $\Rightarrow$ reducing changes what's expressible $\Rightarrow$ a real taxonomy
result. \textbf{Order: full first} (transparent, no cherry-picking).

\begin{openbox}
\textbf{Graded-axis existence test --- AUC vs correlation.} A graded axis (winding, prominence)
may get a \emph{correlation} existence test (projection vs vote-fraction, Spearman/permutation)
rather than AUC --- but that is the \emph{same measurement} as the uncertainty geometry for that
feature, so they may collapse into one thing. Binary features keep AUC regardless. Decide before
wiring Scheme~2.
\end{openbox}

% ===================== 8. CONTROLS =====================
\section{Controls and guardrails}\label{sec:controls}

These are what separate ``we measured something real'' from ``the probe found a shortcut.''
\emph{Attribution is the whole game}: a rung on the ladder (\S\ref{sec:ladder}) means something
about \emph{the images} only once the confounds are held down. The battery is \textbf{tiered} to
protect a small Paper~1 --- must-haves are non-negotiable, the rest defer to Paper~2.

\subsection{Must-have (Paper 1)}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
\item \dec{} \textbf{Selectivity (Hewitt--Liang).} Real-label probe performance \emph{minus}
control-label performance. A direction counts only if it beats a control task --- this is the
guard against probe capacity: a powerful enough probe decodes \emph{anything} (even random
labels), so claim strength is tied to the \emph{gap} over a control, not raw performance.
\item \dec{} \textbf{Negative controls.} Concepts that should \emph{not} be clean directions:
shuffled vote fractions, random labels, random embeddings, image-metadata bins, sky-background /
noise level. The ladder is credible precisely when these \emph{fail} --- ``bars linear; winding
nonlinear; random labels fail; survey identity low after homogenisation'' is the shape of a
trustworthy result.
\item \dec{} \textbf{Core nuisance probes.} Can the same embedding-axis machinery read off
\emph{redshift}, \emph{apparent magnitude}, \emph{angular size} (and survey, if multi-survey)?
If a ``morphology'' direction is really tracking size or brightness, this catches it. This is
\emph{the} reason the battery exists: an MLP that ``decodes winding'' may be decoding apparent
size, SNR, or survey, not morphology.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Deferred (Paper 2 / if feasible)}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \dfr{} \textbf{Matched evaluation.} Re-test on galaxies matched in redshift / size /
brightness, where sample size allows --- the strongest form of nuisance control.
\item \dfr{} \textbf{Cross-split robustness.} Learn the direction on one split, test on another;
ideally train on SDSS, test on DESI/DECaLS where labels later overlap (needs multi-survey ---
Paper~2).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The bar.} A feature is ``linearly nameable'' only if the logistic direction clears
\textbf{AUC $+$ selectivity $+$ the nuisance battery}, not AUC alone. The eigen-triangulation of
the entanglement analysis (\S\ref{sec:harness}) is the most rigorous machinery in the design and
does not depend on the uncertainty-geometry result landing --- so the controls plus the ladder
plus the v1 comparison form a backbone that is solid whatever the high-beta headlines do.

% ===================== 9. STATISTICS =====================
\section{Statistics --- the five decisions, owned}\label{sec:stats}


The statistical layer is four readings of one picture (a real value vs a chance distribution,
``is it past the tail?'') plus a size check. All five flagged decisions are now owned with a
defensible rationale.

\begin{decidedbox}
\textbf{(1) Existence p-value.} Real value (AUC for binary; correlation for graded, pending the
open question) vs a null, at the effect floor. Same add-one estimator as (4), so the same
tie convention applies; \opn{} remains only the AUC $\approx 1.0$ ceiling case.

\textbf{(2) Effect floor.} A single pre-registered minimum AUC; the scientific
``how strong to call a direction real'' call. \opn{} value to be set by -M with rationale.

\textbf{(3) Multiplicity --- Benjamini--Yekutieli (FDR).} Chosen knowingly: the existence tests
are correlated by construction (bulge levels partition one variable; features co-occur), so
independence-assuming methods are wrong; BY controls the false-\emph{discovery} rate (the right
target for a discovery catalogue) under arbitrary dependence. Family count per-scheme
($\approx$37 full, $\approx$10--13 reduced).

\textbf{(4) Permutation test} (uncertainty geometry, possibly graded-existence): shuffle vote
fractions, recompute Spearman, locate the real value in the shuffle null. $\geq$10{,}000
shuffles, two-tailed. \dec{} \textbf{tie-handling: add-one, ties counted into the tail} --- the
attainable floor is $1/(n+1)$, never zero (Phipson \& Smyth), and ties count against the observed
value, which is the conservative direction. Implemented.

\textbf{(5) Marchenko--Pastur null} (entanglement eigenvalues): eigenvalue past the MP edge
computed for the actual matrix shape. ``Past the edge'' to start; Tracy--Widom p-value if a
reviewer wants hard significance.
\end{decidedbox}

% ===================== THE HARNESS (locked design) =====================
\section{The probing harness (locked experimental design)}\label{sec:harness}

\begin{decidedbox}
\textbf{Hub note.} The full derivations live in the authoritative
\texttt{probing-harness-design.md} (586 lines). This section carries the \emph{actual locked
decisions and machinery} at build fidelity --- not a summary --- because the harness \emph{is}
the experimental design (the science), and a reader should not have to open a second document to
learn what the controls test or why the eigen-triangulation works. The harness doc remains the
source for the full derivations; this section must not contradict it.
\end{decidedbox}

\subsection{Two kinds of ``locked''}
\dec{} The design distinguishes \emph{structure-locked} (the architecture of a test is fixed)
from \emph{formula-flagged} (a specific statistical constant or null-application is -M's to own
before finalising, \S\ref{sec:stats}). Everything below is structure-locked; the formula-flagged
points are marked.

\subsection{Sub-system 3 --- the controls battery}\label{sub:controls-battery}

\subsubsection{3A --- the hard gate (the keystone)}
\dec{} The rung verdict is \textbf{not a human reading the numbers} --- it is a
\textbf{pre-registered deterministic function of the controls}, emitted in code, applied
identically to every feature, thresholds fixed \emph{before} looking and stamped. Built on the
existing \texttt{Gate} machinery. The MLP specifically \emph{cannot} return an R3 verdict unless
selectivity \emph{and} nuisance-clearance pass --- enforced structurally.

\emph{Why:} controls only protect you if they \emph{gate the conclusion}, not if they merely
\emph{accompany} it. If a human assigns the rung after seeing the numbers, ``R1'' encodes a
researcher degree of freedom and the apparatus is decorative. \emph{Cost (accepted):} a feature
can land just below the bar and be forced to a rung you ``feel'' is wrong --- that is the point;
it is what makes the result honest.

\textbf{The boundary protocol} (sub-threshold-but-feels-present features): when the gate says R4
for a feature intuition says is present, the response is \emph{not} to argue the verdict down. It
is that \textbf{pre-registered alternative-hypothesis tests fire automatically}, each testing a
\emph{named} alternative, each gated on its \emph{own} controls: a controlled MLP (R3 --- but
only if it clears its own selectivity + nuisance); the resolution/patch-size ablation
(under-resolved, not absent); cross-objective (R4 in JEPA but recoverable in MAE/MoCo $=$ an
objective effect). If a pre-registered test recovers it (clearing its controls), the feature
moves rung \emph{with a named mechanism}. If none do, it is reported honestly as R4 --- and that
is a \emph{possible finding}: human-label confidence exceeding image information, the
v1-confusion-explained-by-pixels story, the paper's reason for existing. \textbf{The discipline:}
``prove it another way'' is legitimate \emph{only} when the other way is a pre-registered control
testing a named alternative --- never ``try seventeen probe architectures until one clears the
bar.'' The rescue-routes are fixed in advance.

\subsubsection{3B --- null-calibrated thresholds + effect-size floor}
\dec{}/\opn{}(formula) Thresholds are \emph{not arbitrary constants}. \textbf{Existence verdict
$=$ exceeds the negative-control null at $p<0.05$} --- the bar for ``real'' is set relative to
what the negative controls achieve, adapting to sample size and probe capacity automatically.
\textbf{Plus a pre-registered effect-size floor} for ``clean vs marginal'' among real effects
(guards the opposite failure: a significant-but-trivial AUC 0.54 at large $N$). The floor is a
pre-registered constant, acceptable \emph{because it no longer does the existence work} ---
significance-vs-null decides real/not-real, the floor only decides clean-vs-marginal.
\textbf{Build-order dependency:} the negative-control null is an \emph{input to the gate
function} --- the gate cannot fire until the null is computed (negatives $\rightarrow$ null
$\rightarrow$ real features gated against it).

\subsubsection{3C --- the negative-control battery (five nulls)}
\dec{} Existence is calibrated against the \textbf{max (most conservative) across all five} ---
a real feature must beat the \emph{strongest} null. Each closes a distinct attack:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Shuffled vote fractions} --- same distribution, destroyed correspondence. Kills
``the probe exploits label marginals, not image content.''
\item \textbf{Random embeddings} (Gaussian, matched covariance) --- kills ``any high-D vector
predicts this.''
\item \textbf{Noise images through the real frozen encoder} (matched to pixel-level statistics,
\emph{not} white noise) --- kills ``the encoder imposes structure on anything.'' \emph{The
strong one} --- ``noise images through our encoder give chance AUC'' is a clean statement that
the encoder learned galaxy structure specifically.
\item \textbf{Untrained-encoder embeddings} (frozen random ViT) --- kills ``the probe, not the
pretraining, does the work.'' \emph{The headline ``pretraining mattered'' control} --- ``a random
ViT gets ${\sim}0.5$ on featured-ness, ours gets ${\sim}0.9$.''
\item \textbf{Sky-background / noise-level labels} --- kills ``the probe reads image depth, not
morphology.''
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{3D --- the nuisance battery (five nuisances + triggered matching)}
\dec{} Answers the most likely \emph{real} confound: morphology genuinely correlates with
physical quantities (bars more visible in bigger/closer/brighter galaxies). \textbf{The five}
(all from the metadata join): redshift, apparent magnitude, Petrosian radius (apparent size),
SNR$_r$ (depth), PSF width (seeing). \textbf{Adjudication:} a parallel probe predicts each
nuisance from the embeddings (diagnostic --- shows \emph{whether} there's a concern); when a
nuisance-AUC is comparable-to-or-above the morphology-AUC, that feature \emph{must} be re-tested
on galaxies \textbf{matched} on that nuisance (held constant, so it can't be the signal). The
feature either \emph{survives} (real) or is \emph{marked confounded}. This makes matching
\emph{targeted} (fires only for flagged features --- bounded cost), promoting it from the
scratchpad's ``Paper-2/if-feasible'' to \textbf{Paper-1, targeted}. Every branch is defensible
--- there is no outcome you'd want to hide.

\subsection{Sub-system 2 --- the ladder}\label{sub:ladder-machinery}

\subsubsection{2A --- entanglement (R1 vs R2): eigen-led triangulation \textnormal{\textit{(the strongest machinery)}}}
\dec{} \emph{Not} raw-cosine-vs-arbitrary-threshold (ambiguous between representational
entanglement and world-correlation, probe-dependent, trivially passed since random high-D
vectors are near-orthogonal). Instead: \textbf{several independent measures with different
failure modes, triangulated} --- where they agree, agreement can't be an artefact of any single
method's blind spot; where they disagree, the disagreement \emph{localises the cause}.

\textbf{The eigen spine:} effective rank of the concept-direction Gram matrix $WW^\top$ (``$k$
named concepts span only $k_{\text{eff}}$ effective dims''; reuses the collapse-monitor code);
the \textbf{Marchenko--Pastur null} on that spectrum (significantly more entangled than random,
by random-matrix theory --- a citable null, not a hand-picked threshold); the
\textbf{eigenvectors} of $WW^\top$ (\emph{which} features collapse onto shared axes ---
localises); the embedding-covariance spectrum (the encoder's intrinsic dimensionality, as
context).

\textbf{Cross-checks, each covering an eigen blind spot from a different origin:} the
\textbf{cosine matrix} (the human-readable $WW^\top$ off-diagonals) --- the direct visual bridge
to v1, the embedding-recovered matrix laid against v1's Fig 18/19 human-confusion matrices
(``does the representation entangle what humans confused?'' --- headline Fig 3);
\textbf{logistic-vs-CAV direction disagreement} (genuinely independent --- two different
\emph{definitions} of the direction); \textbf{conditional recoverability (matched)} --- the
\emph{causal} attribution, the \emph{only} measure that resolves representation-vs-world (fired
surgically, only on eigen-flagged pairs).

\begin{decidedbox}
\textbf{Verdict logic (pre-registered --- critical).} Convergence $=$ strong claim;
\textbf{disagreements are interpreted, not averaged}, with the interpretation rules fixed
\emph{before} results (else triangulation collapses into researcher degrees of freedom). E.g.:
eigen-entangled $+$ cosine shows it $+$ logistic-CAV disagree $+$ conditional survives matching
$\rightarrow$ representational entanglement, four methods agree, unassailable. Eigen-entangled
\emph{but} conditional vanishes under matching $\rightarrow$ the entanglement was
\emph{world-correlation (astrophysics)}, not representational --- itself a clean finding,
distinguishable \emph{because} of the causal cross-check. This is the machinery that adjudicates
the bar+arms hard case (\S\ref{sec:confound}) and Framing~B (\S\ref{sec:framing}).
\end{decidedbox}
\opn{}(formula) The MP-null application and significance machinery on the spectrum are -M's to
own; the structure (triangulation + verdict logic) is locked.

\subsubsection{2B/2C --- multiplicity and feature scope}
\dec{} Multiplicity correction is structure-locked, formula per \S\ref{sec:stats} (BY). Feature
scope is the two-scheme experiment of \S\ref{sec:scheme}.

\subsection{Sub-system 4 --- uncertainty geometry (the headline)}\label{sub:uncertainty-machinery}
\dec{} \textbf{Test statistic:} Spearman of projection-distance vs vote-fraction on the held-out
ambiguous middle, with a permutation null (\S\ref{sec:stats}). \textbf{Scope:} gated on R1/R2
(an axis must exist to project along), run on all qualifying features. \textbf{4C --- the v1
comparison is a first-class deliverable, not an aside:} v1's supervised-confusion-mirroring laid
beside v2's label-free-geometry-recovery is the clean hook (\S\ref{sec:uncertainty}).

\subsection{Sub-system 1 --- the full run}\label{sub:fullrun}
\dec{} \textbf{Compute path} is the gate that shapes everything (the medium 40k run vs the
rented scale run). \textbf{1A:} $\beta$ stays 0.5 for the headline; the $\beta$-sweep is a
downstream ablation. \textbf{1B:} free probe-replication always; paid encoder-replication only on
headline features.

\begin{decidedbox}
\textbf{1C --- checkpoint choice: label-blind, NOT best-AUC (the subtle one).} Do \emph{not}
pick the checkpoint that maximises probe AUC --- best-by-AUC lets the \emph{labels} (via the
probe) choose which encoder you keep, which quietly \textbf{breaks the ``encoder never saw
labels'' firewall} (a subtle circularity a sharp reviewer catches). The checkpoint is chosen by a
\textbf{label-blind} rule, pre-registered before looking at any AUC: the final (most-trained)
checkpoint, gated on the collapse trace being stable at the end, else the last-stable checkpoint.
This is the one place the intuitive choice (``best performing'') is subtly \emph{wrong}.
\end{decidedbox}

\subsection{Sub-system 5 --- the headline figures (deliverables)}\label{sub:figures}
\dec{} Specifying the deliverables sub-systems 2--4 \emph{emit}, so figures fall out of the run
rather than being reverse-engineered. \textbf{Fig 1 --- the ladder} (per-feature R1--R4 $+$ AUC

\subsection{New capability this design adds}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]

% ===================== 12. DATA / MULTI-SURVEY (Paper 2) =====================
\section{Data and multi-survey (Paper 2 scope)}\label{sec:data}

\dfr{} Cross-survey transfer is \textbf{Paper 2 / a major ablation, not Paper 1} --- kept out
of the first clean result, because it is its own deep topic (DeepAstroUDA, AstroCLIP) and
because the whole thesis rests on probing being a \emph{trustworthy instrument}.

\subsection{Is ``more data $\rightarrow$ richer representations'' even true here?}

Mostly yes, but \emph{conditionally}. SSL's premise is that scale and diversity buy generality,
and galaxy imaging sits on hundreds of millions of unlabelled cutouts --- the regime JEPA wants,
one 200k set cannot reach. But ``richer'' is not automatic: it is gated on the \emph{new}
variation being \emph{morphological}, not \emph{instrumental}. Add a second survey and if the
dominant new axis the model discovers is ``SDSS-ness vs DESI-ness,'' capacity has been spent
learning the cameras apart. \textbf{That gate is the whole game.}

\subsection{The homogenisation checklist (worst-first)}

Each item is a channel through which \emph{which telescope took the picture} leaks into the
representation --- and JEPA's objective will exploit any leak that makes the masked target
easier to predict from context (survey identity always does, since context and target share a
survey):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Pixel scale (arcsec/pixel).} SDSS ${\sim}0.396''$, DESI Legacy ${\sim}0.262''$,
HST ${\sim}0.05''$, JWST ${\sim}0.03''$. Resizing arrays \emph{looks} like a fix but is not ---
each crop then covers a different patch of sky. Hold \textbf{arcsec/pixel} constant (or
kpc/pixel, which needs a redshift), not pixel count.
\item \textbf{PSF / sharpness.} Ground-based is seeing-limited and blurry; space-based is
diffraction-limited and crisp. A space galaxy is intrinsically sharper than any resampled
ground-based one --- an almost-perfect survey fingerprint.
\item \textbf{Bandpass $+$ morphological k-correction.} \emph{ugriz} vs \emph{grz} vs near-IR
mean different physical things across filter sets; at high $z$ the observed bands sample bluer
rest-frame light, so a high-$z$ JWST galaxy is not even the same physical object as a low-$z$
SDSS one in nominally ``the same'' colours.
\item \textbf{Depth / SNR / noise texture.} Deeper surveys reveal faint tidal features shallow
ones miss; noise statistics differ by instrument.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The design fork --- degrade-down vs preserve-and-correct}

\textbf{Degrade-down:} match everyone to the worst common denominator (coarsest PSF, shallowest
depth, largest pixel scale). Conservative, kills most leakage, yields a genuinely comparable
corpus; cost: throws away the resolution of the good data. \textbf{Preserve-and-correct:} keep
native quality and make the model invariant to the gap (augmentation spanning it, explicit
domain adaptation, or trust scale to wash it out). Higher ceiling, far more to go wrong.

\dec{} \textbf{Recommendation: degrade-down first} --- you cannot trust a direction that might
mean ``barred'' vs ``the survey where bars were easier to see.'' Get the comparable corpus and
honest diagnostics working; \emph{then}, as a deliberate experiment, relax toward
preserve-and-correct and watch leakage and probe quality move.

\subsection{The merge as a controlled experiment}

The survey-leakage probe turns ``did the merge help?'' into a measurement, not a hope. Pretrain
single-survey vs multi-survey; probe morphology on the \emph{same} held-out labelled set both
ways; read \textbf{two} numbers off each: \textbf{morphology probe AUC} (representation quality)
and \textbf{survey-leakage AUC} (instrument contamination). \emph{Clean win:} morphology-AUC
$\uparrow$ \emph{and} leakage-AUC flat/$\downarrow$. \emph{The trap (and the interesting
failure):} morphology-AUC flat/$\downarrow$ while leakage-AUC $\uparrow$ --- the model spent the
extra data on the cameras. Scale to an ablation: plot both AUCs against corpus heterogeneity
(single $\rightarrow$ two close surveys $\rightarrow$ add a space-based one).

\subsection{Connection to the confound taxonomy}

The imaging-depth axis of the confound taxonomy (\S\ref{sec:confound}) lives here:
\textbf{projection-confusion is invariant to imaging depth; resolution-confusion is not.} So the
eventual GZ2 $\rightarrow$ DECaLS/DESI comparison is the instrument that \emph{splits} resolution
from semantic confusion and \emph{tests} projection's depth-invariance --- a falsifiable,
two-direction prediction. \textbf{Sequencing:} SDSS $+$ DESI Legacy first (both ground-based
optical, overlapping bands, homogenisation tractable); the space-vs-ground jump is experiment
two. Don't reach for JWST to prove the point.

\section{Prior art and positioning}\label{sec:priorart}

\begin{openbox}

\subsection{Galaxy SSL --- contrastive, and (newer) masked-reconstruction}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item Hayat et al.\ 2021 --- contrastive (MoCo) SSL on SDSS; downstream morphology + photo-z.
\item Stein et al.\ 2022 --- MoCo for lens-finding.
\item Walmsley et al.\ 2022 --- \emph{Towards Galaxy Foundation Models with Hybrid Contrastive
Learning} (BYOL-style + GZ info; introduces \textbf{GZ-Evo}, ${\sim}552$k labelled +
${\sim}1.34$M comparable unlabelled). (arXiv 2206.11927)
\item Walmsley et al.\ 2023 --- \emph{Galaxy Zoo DESI}, 8.7M galaxies (arXiv 2309.11425);
Zoobot.
\item AstroCLIP (arXiv 2310.03024) --- cross-modal FM, DESI spectra + Legacy images.
\item \textbf{Masked autoencoders (MAE)} for galaxies (Wu et al., below) --- pixel-reconstruction
SSL, the natural foil for JEPA's latent prediction.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Closest neighbour --- interpreting galaxy representations against Galaxy Zoo}
\textbf{Wu et al.\ 2025} --- \emph{Re-envisioning Euclid Galaxy Morphology: Identifying and
Interpreting Features with Sparse Autoencoders} (arXiv 2510.23749). SAEs on supervised (Zoobot)
\emph{and} self-supervised (MAE) embeddings, Euclid Q1; finds interpretable features aligned
\emph{with and beyond} the GZ decision tree, stronger GZ-alignment than PCA. Public code + MAE.
\textbf{Our distinctions:}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{JEPA latent-prediction vs their MAE pixel-reconstruction} --- their MAE is the
ideal head-to-head baseline.
\item \textbf{Top-down hypothesis-testing} (probe a \emph{named} human concept, walk the ladder)
vs \textbf{bottom-up discovery} (read features off an SAE dictionary).
\item \textbf{Uncertainty geometry} --- we test vote-fraction \emph{recovery}, not just label
alignment.
\end{enumerate}
Complementary, not competing (one could run an SAE on the JEPA encoder --- see
\S\ref{sec:parking}).

\subsection{JEPA --- proven adjacent, not (apparently) galaxy morphology}
Assran et al.\ 2023 (I-JEPA); Lens-JEPA (physics-informed gravitational lensing, NeurIPS ML4PS
2025 --- beats supervised + vanilla I-JEPA); HEP-JEPA (collider physics, arXiv 2502.03933).

\subsection{Cross-survey domain gap}
DeepAstroUDA (Ćiprijanović et al., ML:S\&T 2023) --- frames cross-survey morphology as domain
adaptation; warns models extract dataset-specific, non-robust features. Vision-FM-for-astronomy
survey (arXiv 2409.11175) --- FM features helped optical galaxy classification, poor on radio;
transfer isn't free.

\subsection{Probing / concept-direction lineage (the method's ancestry)}
Alain \& Bengio 2016 (linear classifier probes); Kim et al.\ 2018 (TCAV / Concept Activation
Vectors); Hewitt \& Liang 2019 (control tasks + selectivity).

% ===================== RISKS =====================
\section{Risks and unknowns}\label{sec:risks}

The failure modes the design guards against, gathered in one place. Each is either mitigated by a
named control or accepted as a bounded risk.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Spurious-correlate probes (R3).} A high-capacity probe can decode a feature via
nuisance correlates (z, size, brightness, survey, SNR, PSF), not morphology. \emph{The} reason
the controls battery exists (\S\ref{sec:controls}); an MLP result never stands alone --- the hard
gate (\S\ref{sub:controls-battery}, 3A) structurally forbids an R3 verdict without selectivity
$+$ nuisance clearance.
\item \textbf{Probe-capacity confound.} A powerful enough probe decodes \emph{anything} (even
random labels). Claim strength is tied to probe simplicity: linear$+$selective $=$ strongest;
MLP-only $=$ weaker and must be controlled. Guarded by selectivity (the gap over a control) and
the random-label / random-embedding nulls.
\item \textbf{Representation collapse} --- JEPA's standard failure mode. Budget tuning on the EMA
schedule $+$ masking ratio; the effective-rank collapse monitor (\S\ref{sec:harness}) is the
detector, and the label-blind checkpoint rule (1C) is gated on a stable end-of-training trace.
\item \textbf{Survey shortcut} \emph{(multi-survey, Paper 2)} --- instrument identity as the
dominant learned axis. Caught by the survey-leakage probe; mitigated by homogenisation $+$ close
surveys first (\S\ref{sec:data}).
\item \textbf{Compute + orchestration.} Pretraining a ViT from scratch $\gg$ v1's
transfer-learning runs. The EMA-schedule / masking-ratio grid is \emph{modest} for Paper~1, so a
lightweight sweep harness suffices --- \textbf{do not build MLOps before the science is
de-risked}. The heavy declarative-sweep machinery earns its keep only at Paper~2's survey-leakage
ablation. Within FMX hardware, but plan it.
\item \textbf{Null uncertainty geometry.} The highest-upside result is the most likely to come
out null. \dfr{} Backbone the paper on the ladder $+$ controls $+$ v1 comparison, not on this
landing positive (\S\ref{sec:framing}, \S\ref{sec:uncertainty}).
\item \textbf{Scope creep.} Stage~1 $+$ frozen logistic probing $+$ must-have controls $+$ the
three headline figures is \emph{the minimum that answers the question}. Everything else (E(2)
equivariance, multi-survey, fine-tuning, SAE) is explicitly deferred or parked.
\end{itemize}

\section{Open questions register}\label{sec:open}

single register; individual sections above hold the context.) Split into \emph{design}

\textbf{Design questions:}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
\item Graded-axis existence test: AUC vs correlation (possible merge with uncertainty geometry). Before wiring Scheme 2.
\item Conditional-population gating: which upstream vote, which threshold, consensus vs weight. Per-run knob; default to set.
\item Vote-count filter threshold (v1: mean$+2\sigma \approx 21$); re-count usable deep-feature N at threshold.
\item Effect-floor value --- -M's scientific call (clean-vs-marginal; not the existence work).
\item Which axis ratio per population (\texttt{expAB} vs \texttt{deVAB}).
\item Backbone size (ViT-S/16 to start?); from-scratch vs ImageNet warm-start (lean from-scratch); masking specifics; E(2)-equivariance ablation timing.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Formula-flagged (structure locked, formula to own):}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt,start=7]
\item \textbf{Existence significance machinery} (\S\ref{sub:controls-battery}, 3B) --- the specific null-calibrated test and its multiplicity-corrected (BY) form.
\item \textbf{The five-null combination leaves the null degenerate} (\S\ref{sub:controls-battery},
3C) --- \emph{measured}, not conjectured. Three of the five controls (noise-through-encoder,
untrained-encoder, sky/noise) are single AUCs rather than distributions; only shuffled-labels and
random-embeddings resample. Since 3C calibrates against the \emph{max} across all five, and the
largest singleton exceeds every resampled draw, the combined null has zero variance --- so the
existence $p$ can only be $1/(n+1)$ or $1$, and the draw count buys no resolution. Candidates
(recorded, none chosen): a seeded \emph{ensemble} of untrained encoders (measured spread across 8
seeds: AUC $0.648$--$0.702$, $\sigma = 0.018$, $\approx$5.6\,min per seed to embed a 40k corpus);
resampling the remaining singletons; or a combination rule that keeps the singletons as a
threshold rather than folding them into the sampled null.
\item \textbf{Marchenko--Pastur null application} on the concept-direction spectrum (\S\ref{sub:ladder-machinery}, 2A) --- the edge computation for the actual matrix shape, and whether to add the Tracy--Widom significance.
\item \textbf{Selectivity ceiling} (\S\ref{sub:ladder-machinery}, the MLP rung) --- the
``control exceeds its own null'' predicate, which is \emph{not} one of the five and has no
mapping onto them. \textbf{Candidate (recorded, not decided):} give the MLP rung its own null
from the \emph{untrained-encoder} control rather than a fixed constant, so selectivity is
relative in the same way every other rung already is and the ladder stays internally consistent.
\item Tie-handling: existence p-value (AUC$\approx$1) --- \textbf{OPEN}. The permutation-test
side is \textbf{DECIDED}: the add-one estimator counts ties into the tail, so the attainable
floor is $1/(n+1)$ rather than $1/n$ --- a permutation p-value should never be zero
(Phipson \& Smyth), and counting ties against the observed value is the conservative direction.
\item Permutation shuffle count and one- vs two-tailed for the uncertainty geometry (lean $\geq$10{,}000, two-tailed).
\end{enumerate}
