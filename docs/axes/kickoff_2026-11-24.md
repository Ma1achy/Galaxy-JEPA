## Part H: is the information there but our readout misses it? Where does it live, and what causes it?

Every analysis runs on A1 **and** the 3 untrained seeds (same architecture), plus a shuffled-label
control wherever a probe is fitted. A result counts only where A1 clearly beats untrained.

Targets throughout: the 30 STRONG/SEPARATE axes, Part A's ordinal axes (arm count, winding, bulge,
roundness), and the EFIGI attributes from G2 where matched.

**Storage.** Saving all 12 blocks' tokens for 35k galaxies is about 80 GB, too much.
- Save per-layer mean-pooled vectors (12 × 384) for all probe galaxies.
- Save full token grids only for a 6k test subset (stratified by resolution decile) at blocks 4, 8, 10
  and 12.
- Report disk and memory before running.

**H1. Readout: the mean against the tokens** (one forward pass, shared with H2a).
- On the final block's tokens, three readouts:
  - (a) the mean-pooled probe (the current readout);
  - (b) a 4×4 spatial-pooled grid probe (16 × 384, strong L2);
  - (c) an attention-pooling probe (one learned query, ≤ 50k parameters, early stopping on a validation
    split).
- The same split and the same probes on the untrained tokens. Attention pooling can extract a lot even
  from random features, so the A1-minus-untrained gain is the number that matters.
- Report AUC (or the LL gap and τ for ordinal targets) per readout, the gain over the mean probe with CI,
  and the breakdown by resolution decile.
- Reading: if arm count or winding gains clearly more for A1 than for untrained, the information was in
  the tokens and mean pooling hid it.

**H2. Layers and circuits.**
- **H2a. Layer sweep.** Mean-pooled probes at every block, for all targets.
  - Report where each feature emerges (the first block clearing untrained by 0.05 on its CI) and where
    it peaks.
  - Do first-order features form earlier than structure and fine detail?
- **H2b. Location maps.** On the token subset, per-token linear probes at blocks 4, 8, 10 and 12 for
  bulge, bar, spiral/arms and edge-on.
  - Output: average maps of where each feature is represented (centre, disc, outskirts).
  - Sanity check: the bulge should be central and the arms outside it.
- **H2c. Activation patching (causal).**
  - Pairs: test galaxies differing in one feature (bar vs no bar; spiral vs smooth; dominant vs no
    bulge), matched on the five nuisances (nearest neighbours in nuisance space; report match quality).
  - Intervention: run the target galaxy with the residual stream at block L replaced by the source
    galaxy's in one region (all tokens; the central 4×4; the outer ring), then let the rest of the
    network run.
  - Measure the change in that feature's final probe score, and in 3 unrelated features (specificity).
  - Sweep L = 1…12 × region. Null: patching between two galaxies that both lack the feature.
  - At the block where patching matters most, patch individual attention heads' outputs to find the
    heads that carry the feature.
  - Report patching-effect heatmaps (block × region, and per head), A1 against untrained.
- **H2d. Later, not now:** sparse autoencoders per block, with features named against Part B and EFIGI.

**H3. Counterfactual edits** (after Part B; needs the Simard parameters). Effects are reported in test-SD
units, as a specificity matrix (edit × axis), A1 against untrained.
- **Bulge removal:** render each galaxy's Simard bulge model (Sérsic, PSF-convolved, in the stamp's units)
  and subtract it. Measure the movement along every axis.
  - Prediction: the bulge axes fall; edge-on and roundness barely move.
  - Control: subtract the same flux as a smooth ring at the disc's scale length.
- **Arm injection:** on galaxies with smooth discs, add a logarithmic-spiral modulation of the fitted
  disc (m = 2, 3, 4; pitch 10°, 20°, 30°; fixed arm contrast). Measure the movement along spiral, arm count,
  winding and the EFIGI arm axes.
  - Control: the same modulation phase-scrambled (same flux, no spiral).
- **Planted check on every edit:** an edit with zero amplitude must leave the embedding unchanged
  (cosine ≈ 1).

**H4. Disagreement mining.**
- Select galaxies where A1's cleaned probe confidently disagrees with confident votes: the top 1% of
  |score| in the wrong direction, with reach ≥ median and vote agreement ≥ 0.8. Features: spiral, bar,
  bulge prominence, edge-on.
- Judge them with independent references where matched: EFIGI, Nair & Abraham, and GZ DECaLS votes
  (deeper images).
- Report the fraction where the reference sides with A1 against GZ2, against the reference's base rate on
  a random sample of agreeing galaxies. Sheets of 32 per feature.

**H5. A supervised ceiling: Zoobot.**
- Find the public Zoobot weights (Walmsley et al.). Record the version, the licence and **what it was
  trained on**.
- If its training set includes GZ2 or our test galaxies, report the overlap. Results on overlapping
  galaxies are leaked: show them separately or exclude them.
- Embed our test stamps with Zoobot's own preprocessing. State the conversion from our g/r/i and the domain
  shift from its training imaging.
- Run the 30 axes, Part A's ordinal axes and B1's decile curves.
- Reading: if Zoobot also can't read arm count in SDSS's low deciles, the limit is the data, not
  self-supervision.

**H6. Synthetic galaxies with known parameters (GalSim).**
- Grid: bulge fraction (0–0.8), bulge Sérsic n (1–4), disc inclination (0–80°), arm count (0, 2, 3, 4),
  pitch (10–35°), arm contrast (3 levels), apparent size (deciles 3, 6, 9).
- Rendering: 0.396″/px with the median SDSS PSF and sky noise, colours from typical bulge/disc SEDs,
  through our exact stretch and normalisation. Check rendered pixel statistics against real stamps.
- Embed with A1 and the untrained seeds. For each parameter, report:
  - the direction from regressing the embedding on it (the other parameters fixed by design);
  - its cosine and score correlation with the real-data directions (GZ2 ordinal, EFIGI, Simard);
  - whether the real probes order the synthetic galaxies correctly.
- Report the domain gap plainly (the nearest-real-neighbour distance for the synthetics).
